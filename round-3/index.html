1 · A new YouTube upload refreshes the content and rebuilds the site by itself

  • tools/sync-videos.mjs reads the channel feed, merges it into youtube-archive.json, downloads missing thumbnails, and reports changed=true|false.
  • production-plan/sync-videos.yml (proposed GitHub Actions workflow) runs it every 30 minutes and on demand. When changed is true it commits the archive and the thumbnail as Ken and pushes; the push is what makes Vercel rebuild, as for any commit. When nothing is new there is no commit and no build.
  • Cost: $0. kenashe/kenashe is a public repository, so Actions minutes are free. No webhook endpoint, server or paid service. Kill switch: repo variable VIDEO_SYNC_ENABLED=false.
  • Typical delay from upload to live: up to 30 minutes for the next run plus the 8 to 10 minute build; the "Run workflow" button skips the wait.
  • Evidence: production-plan/evidence/live-sync-run.txt is a real run against Ken's live feed, starting from an archive that lacked his newest video; the run added it, stored the link in its description (/building/autonomous-ai-blog), downloaded its 109 KB thumbnail, and a second run changed nothing. Test 1 covers the same flow against a local stand-in for YouTube.

2 · The video archive outlives the feed's 15-item window

The feed only ever lists the latest 15 uploads. The archive file is append-only and committed with the site, so a video stays on the site after it leaves the feed. Links found in descriptions are stored in the archive too, so relationships survive as well. Test 2 pushes 28 videos through a 15-item feed in three steps and checks that all 28 remain. The real archive already holds 16 (one more than the feed).

3 · Thumbnails are acquired with no manual step

For every archived video without a thumbnail, the sync tries maxresdefault, then sddefault, then hqdefault, rejects YouTube's tiny grey placeholder, saves the file (WebP via sharp, which Astro already installs; JPEG otherwise) and records its path. A failed download leaves thumbnail: null and is retried on every later run. While it is null the site draws the item's data-derived visual instead of a broken image (item.image === null is a designed state). Tests 3 and 5.

5 · New kinds of work without editing theme rules

Round 2 filed everything with five fixed keyword rules. Round 3 keeps those as a seed vocabulary and adds four mechanisms, tried in this order (classify() in lib/graph.js):

  1. topic: an optional topic: 'Hardware' on an entry. Typed once, only when Ken wants control.
  2. rule: the seed vocabulary (Film & video, Onchain, Web & search, Agents & automation).
  3. stack: a build with no keyword match goes where earlier builds with the same stack tags were filed.
  4. emerged: when four or more still-unfiled items share a tag (a stack tag, or a video hashtag), that tag becomes a theme by itself.
  5. linked: a video or essay with no other signal takes the theme of the work it links to.
  6. otherwise Life & curiosity.

Themes are a dynamic list. Each gets an arc of the universe sized by how much work it holds, so a new theme makes its own room and existing work stays in its own theme (test 6). The build reports how every item was filed (stats.filedBy), and the design can show it as a receipt. In the stress data, "Robotics", "3D printing" and "Homelab" emerge without any rule, and "Hardware" appears from a topic.

Today's real data: 34 items filed by rule, 10 in Life & curiosity; no item needs a topic.

6 · The universe with hundreds or thousands of personal items

See "Future-scale stress test" in the Round 3 report for measured numbers. The architecture side:

  • graph.clusters('year'|'quarter'|'month') summarises personal work by theme and period. The renderer draws clusters when there are too many items for the view and individual lights for the focused region.
  • Positions stay computed: angle from the theme's arc, time from the date. Nothing is positioned by hand at any scale.
  • item.priority (connections, substance, recency) decides which labels are shown.
  • The Digest is summarised per week (digest.perWeek): 1,399 posts are 29 values today; a 55,179-post Digest is under 900.
  • Search runs over a compact prebuilt index (data/search.json); 3,004 items search in about 1 ms.
  • Graph build time: 10 ms for today's 44 items, about 120 ms for 3,004 (test 7).

Design and architecture report

One prototype: The Living Universe, evolved from Round 2 Direction B, with Direction A's personal identity and Direction C's discovery tools. Private design exploration; production untouched.

The design in one paragraph

Ken's photograph stands as a lit window at "now", the centre of a universe made only of his work. The newest pieces sit nearest to him; everything older recedes outward. His name, positioning, newest video and two calls to action share that first screen. Conventional navigation, three index pages and search sit beside the universe, so it is a signature experience and never a gate.

Decisions and why

Identity inside the universe. The portraits have bright office backgrounds and may not be cut out or altered, so the photograph is used honestly as a rectangular object in the scene: a window that emits the universe's light. Essays are lit from it, the plane brightens under it, work on the far side passes behind it. This ties the person to the data (he is literally where "now" is) without repeating Direction A's circular portrait in rings. The same window appears in each item page's small sky.

The Digest, subordinate. In Round 2 its 1,399 grains were the brightest shape in the sky. Now it is a single desaturated band under the plane, drawn from weekly totals in at most 32 segments, labelled "Automated Digest · paused" from the derived status, never counted with personal work, and expandable on demand. On index pages it is one quiet line; on its own build page the status reads "Publishing paused" from the same source. If publishing resumes, the wording changes everywhere with no edit.

One signature interaction. Select a light, follow a thread, replay growth. Direction C's playhead, monitor and tracks were not brought over. What was brought over is function: the latest-video feature, the archive, search and filters, receipts and threads.

Typography and hierarchy. Instrument Serif for the name, headings and Ken's own voice; Hanken Grotesk for reading and interface (already a production font); JetBrains Mono only for hashes. A hard floor of 14 px, body at 17 px. The automated checks measure the smallest rendered text on every page.

Homepage. First screen, latest video with its threads and more to watch, newest builds with receipts and thread counts, the film, latest writing, who Ken is, a small Digest strip, footer. 5.0 desktop viewports, 7.4 on a phone.

Level of detail. Whole recent months as individual lights up to a budget; older work as clusters by month, quarter and year, opened by selecting or zooming. Time maps to radius logarithmically from the window's foot, so one year looks almost linear and sixteen years still leave the newest year about half the radius.

Progressive enhancement. Prerendered SVG poster with real links (what JavaScript-off and ?tier=still show), then Canvas 2D, then a lazy raw WebGL light layer on capable devices. No graphics library.

Design decisions

1. Identity: Ken is where the light comes from

The first screen is one composition, not a hero in front of a backdrop.

  • The photograph is a lit window standing at "now". The universe's time axis has t = 1 (the present) at its centre. Ken's current headshot stands exactly there, as an honest rectangle (CSS crop and box-shadow only; nothing touches his face or removes the office background). The bright office behind him is used as what it is: the brightest thing in a night sky. A pool of warm light falls from it onto the plane, essays are drawn as planets whose lit side faces it, and the inner ring of the time axis is drawn in the same window colour. The caption under it reads Now · Nov 25, 2026 from graph.time.end.
  • It is a real object in the scene. The window stands on the plane, so work on the far side passes behind it and is really occluded (not labelled, not clickable) until the slow drift or a selection brings it round. Nothing is placed inside the present: the time-to-radius mapping starts at R0, the foot of the window.
  • The name is a title. "Ken Ashe" is set in Instrument Serif at up to 168px with a faint warm gradient toward the window, next to the photograph; the tagline (verbatim) is serif italic, the subline (verbatim) is plain text.
  • The newest video is the newest light. graph.latestVideo is announced inside the hero as a small card, tied by a dotted leader to its actual body in the universe. "Watch the latest video" is the one amber action; it scrolls to the feature and starts the player. "Explore the universe" and YouTube / X follow links complete the calls to action.
  • Not Direction A's device. No circular portrait, no rings around a face; the portrait is rectangular, stands on a tilted plane, and the universe is not centred on the page but on him.
  • Mobile is its own layout. Positioning line, the name at 92px, then the window standing in the universe at full width, then the tagline and the primary action: all inside the first 844px screen even with the prototype's tall concept bar. Subline, the latest-video card and follow links are in the second screen. Controls collapse to three icon buttons at the side of the stage; the selection card becomes a bottom sheet.
2. The Digest is an undercurrent, not a body

Round 2 drew one grain per post (1,399 grains: the largest, brightest shape). Now:

  • It is not a light and not counted. The digest item has no body; headline counts and the poster title count only builds, essays, videos and shorts.
  • It is one faint band lying 0.03 units below the plane inside the Web & search sector, built from graph.digest.perWeek: each week's posts are spread over the radial interval that week occupies and binned into at most 32 segments. Opacity is normalised to the densest segment and capped (≤ 0.14 at rest), the tint is ash (#8a939d paused, a dull warm #b3a58f when publishing), and it is drawn before everything else.
  • Constant cost and constant weight at any volume. 1,399 posts → 25 segments; 55,179 posts → 32 segments. With the Digest publishing for 16 years it is still a haze under the work (see stress/3000-digest/).
  • A 14px label, Automated Digest · paused, derived from graph.digest.status, sits at the band's outer end. Hovering or selecting it (or "Show the Digest" on /universe/) reveals the detail on demand: the band lifts, one hairline per published week appears, and a card shows the shared Digest status block (derived status line, counts, date range, noindex, weekly sparkline, archive link, link to the build that explains it). If the status were publishing the label, the tint and a slow shimmer along the band would say so.
  • On the page it is one compact ash strip with a dashed border, after everything of Ken's, and a small column in the footer; in the nav it carries an "automated" tag.
3. Time to radius

rho(t) = R0 + (1 − R0) · log1p(age / τ) / log1p(span / τ) with age = (tNow − t) · span, R0 = 0.235, τ = 30 days (scene.js → timeModel). t is item.layout.t.

  • With one year of work (today) it is close to linear but gives recent months more room: the newest 44 days, which hold half of all items, get 36 % of the radius.
  • With sixteen years the newest year still owns about half of the radius and every older year is compressed a little more; nothing is ever dropped off the disk. Work older than the modern record (the 2016 essay) sits on the rim, which is labelled "Before Nov 2025".
  • The time axis adapts: quarter rings while the record is under about 2.5 years, year rings after that.
  • Replay growth runs tNow from 0 to 1. Everything is placed where it was at that moment, so the universe literally grows outward from Ken. The pace is half calendar, half item count, so busy months get the time they deserve. The same slider scrubs manually (arrow keys step one month).

5. The universe: lighting, depth, motion
  • Kinds of light: builds are stars with diffraction spikes (LIVE builds twinkle slightly), essays are planets lit from now, videos are ringed bodies, shorts smaller ringed bodies. Colour is the theme (from theme.hue), shape is the kind.
  • Depth: every light stands on a hairline stalk above the plane; atmospheric perspective fades and warms distant lights; depth of field widens and softens halos away from the plane of focus (in the shader on WebGL, by sprite size on Canvas).
  • Motion, all data-driven and pausable: a very slow drift, a light travelling along each thread in its documented direction, twinkle on LIVE builds, shimmer on the Digest only when publishing.
  • Selecting on the homepage turns the universe like a turntable around Ken and brings the piece to the front; on /universe/ the camera flies to it and makes room for the card. Follow a thread sends a bright light along the documented link while the camera travels with it, then selects the other end.
  • The card: image or data-derived visual, kind, status, date, theme, receipts count, thread count, dek, one clear link, and one button per documented thread with its evidence.
6. Tiers
  1. Poster (poster.js): prerendered SVG, real links, works with JS off; also what ?tier=still shows.
  2. Canvas 2D (universe.js): everything interactive.
  3. WebGL (gl.js, 3 KB, raw, lazy): an additive light layer under the 2D canvas. Loaded only when not reduced motion, not Save-Data, more than 2 cores and more than 2 GB; if it fails or loses its context, Canvas 2D keeps drawing.

?tier=still|canvas|webgl forces a tier. DPR is capped at 2 (1.5 on low-power devices and for the WebGL layer); low-power devices run at 30 fps. The loop stops when the stage is off-screen, the tab is hidden, motion is paused or nothing is changing (measured: 0 frames/s paused, off-screen and under reduced motion).

7. Accessibility of the universe
  • JS off: the poster's 44 lights are ordinary links with titles; all content sections are plain HTML.
  • JS on: the universe is one tab stop (role="application" with instructions in its label). Arrow keys travel to the nearest item in that direction on screen, Enter opens the selected item (or opens a cluster), Escape clears, +/− zoom, Home selects the newest, and on /universe/ L jumps to the list view. Each selection is announced through a polite live region ("Build: ZeroHuman, Nov 2026, 2 documented threads. Press Enter to open.") and opens the card, whose thread buttons and link are ordinary controls.
  • List view on /universe/: the complete text equivalent, by year, with a "Show in universe" button per item, plus every documented thread with its evidence. The homepage links to it ("Explore the universe", "Full screen").
  • Touch: tap selects, horizontal drag orbits, pinch zooms. On the homepage the stage is touch-action: pan-y, so a vertical swipe that starts on the universe scrolls the page (verified with synthetic touch events); the mouse wheel never zooms there. On /universe/ the stage takes all gestures and the wheel zooms toward the pointer.
  • Reduced motion: no drift, no travelling lights, no shimmer; camera and labels change instantly; replay does not autoplay, the slider steps through time instead. A Pause control is always present. Replay and time slider are a button and a native range input with aria-valuetext.
  • Contrast: text 17.4:1, secondary 12.3:1, muted 7.7:1 on the background; canvas labels have a dark outline. No text is under 14px in HTML, SVG or canvas (checked by script on every page at 390, 768 and 1440px, JS on and off).

How the pages work

One model for three indexes

INDEX in regions/discovery.js describes each index: which item types it lists, which URL parameters it has, how it matches and groups. itemsOf → groupsOf → resultsHTML turns the graph into grouped HTML. The prerender calls these functions with no filters; the page script calls the same functions when a filter changes. On load the script adopts the prerendered view untouched (no re-render, no flash) unless the URL asks for a filtered view.

Nothing is typed by hand: counts, dates, themes (from graph.themes), statuses, stack tags, year and month groups, the latest video and the Digest state all come from the graph. ?demo=video,build,category was checked: the placeholder video becomes the feature and a dashed, labelled card; placeholder builds appear in the project index.

Global search
  • Opens from the header button (r3:search, preventDefault() called), /, and Ctrl/Cmd+K.
  • Native <dialog> with showModal(): focus is trapped, the page behind is inert, Escape closes, focus returns to the control that opened it. If showModal is missing the script does not claim the event and the link works.
  • Nothing is fetched until first open. On the real site it then fetches data/search.json and searches it with search() from lib/graph.js. Pages on other data (the stress pages, ?demo=) search their own graph with searchIndex(graph).
  • Kind filters (All, Builds, Videos, Shorts, Writing) carry live counts for the current query. Empty query lists the newest work. 20 rows at a time, "Show 20 more" inside the list.
  • Rows: thumbnail or data-derived visual, title, kind and status, theme, date; a second link "Show in the universe" (universe/#i=<id>; on stress pages the stress page that shows that dataset).
  • Keyboard: roving focus over real links. Down from the field enters the list, Up and Down move, Right and Left switch between a row's two links, Home and End jump, Enter in the field opens the top result, typing anywhere in the list goes back to the field. I chose roving focus over aria-activedescendant because each row holds two links, which a listbox option may not contain.
  • The footer says plainly that the automated Digest is not searched and links to its archive.
Without JavaScript
  • All three pages are complete: every card and row is in the HTML, video titles link to YouTube, builds and essays to their pages. Controls that need scripts are hidden; the year (or month) chips remain as anchor links to the groups.
  • At scale, a section's first 12 cards (24 rows) are rendered while a budget of 60 lasts; the remainder is a plain list of links inside <noscript>, each with its v-<videoId> id. With scripts off that is the full archive as a readable list (about 2,100 links on the stress page); with scripts on, <noscript> content is not parsed into elements, so it costs no DOM nodes.
  • The header Search button is a normal link to /building/.
Keeping 2,000+ videos pleasant
  • Filtering is an in-memory pass over the graph (1 to 3 ms for 2,128 videos). Only the first two screenfuls are written to the DOM on a filter change.
  • Sections are windowed above 120 items in an index: a section holds cards only while it is within 900px of the viewport. Later groups start empty and fill on approach; sections scrolled away are parked with their height kept, so the page does not jump. "Show 48 more" adds 48 at a time. The real site (16 videos) is below the threshold, so everything stays in the page and in-page find keeps working.

  2. Receipts sit where the claim is. Three forms, one visual language: receipt slips (the quoted request and reply, the facts the page states, and the matching transactions side by side), margin receipts (from 1200px the transaction sits in the margin beside the sentence it backs) and the full Receipts list near the end.   5. The film is a film. First screen: "A short film by Ken Ashe · 2026 · 1:54 runtime", the title in serif italic, the logline, and the film sized to fit the screen, click to load.   • The same build says "Live" elsewhere. The lead's kicker() and the Building index print item.status, so the Autonomous AI blog reads "Live" on cards while its own page reads "Publishing paused". Moving statusOf() into components.js would make them agree. I did not edit the lead's file.
