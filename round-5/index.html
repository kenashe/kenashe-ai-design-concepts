This round recomposes the phone introduction, gives projects without pictures a proper presentation, polishes the page transition, and fixes the update and editorial problems found in review. It is still a design concept: production was only read, nothing was deployed there, and Rounds 1 to 4 are unchanged in the same repository.

What changed, mapped to your brief

1. Positioning and visual direction. The approved wording is used exactly and is checked by a test: name, descriptor, statement, personality line, introduction, both actions. Palette, typefaces, photographs, light reading surfaces and the four navigation items are unchanged. "Videos" is used throughout; Returning Your Things is one project described as a short film.

2. Phone introduction. Your portrait now sits beside your name at the top of the page. The header is one line. The personality line is smaller and lighter than the statement (19 px against 26 px; it was 23 px). The two actions are full-width, 48 px tall. 3. Projects without pictures. The coloured title tiles are gone. A project with no picture is a text card: a small mint mark, the date, the title once, the summary, and "Read about it". Its page opens straight into the heading, summary and write-up, with no substitute cover. Real images and video thumbnails are kept. Both kinds sit in one list in date order. A cover image stays optional when you add a project. The homepage rule no longer requires a picture (see "How the homepage chooses" below); ZeroHuman now appears there.

4. Character and the one interaction. The large headings use a slightly narrower cut of the same typeface. The mint mark from your logo is now a recurring device: it marks text cards and travels into the page as a short rule above the heading, the way a picture travels for illustrated projects. The transition itself is tidier: the old page fades quickly, the new one rises in underneath the travelling picture. On a mouse, a card's picture lifts and tips very slightly when pointed at. Back was verified; reduced motion switches all of it off; without the transition, links load normally.

5. Update and editorial fixes. Each has a regression test (23 tests pass).

  • A. Thumbnails. The sync now downloads the thumbnail of every video in the feed on every run and compares its fingerprint with the stored one. A changed picture replaces the local copy. A download is accepted only if it is a complete, valid image; an error page, a cut-off file or a failed request leaves the last good picture in place. A thumbnail-only change and a title-only change are tested separately.
  • B. Latest and Featured. The automatic newest video is labelled "Latest video". Anything you choose is labelled "Featured video", "Featured project" or "Featured essay". The date shown is always the selected item's own.
  • C. Homepage selections. A project with a page links to it. A project with no write-up is shown as an unlinked card. A mistyped id is ignored. An item that is also hidden is left out. In the last two cases the build prints a plain message and falls back to the automatic choice. No setting can produce a broken link.
  • D. One source per field. The plain summaries now live in each project's record. See the field table below.
  • E. Real project update. Demonstrated on the real production files in an isolated copy. See demo/.

6. Copy. The blog video's description on the site now reads "A look back at my autonomous AI blog and its results. Publishing is currently paused." The blog project's summary is in the past tense and says the same. Lucky Domains is "a digital presence studio for the AI era" (footer: "Digital presence studio."). "All written by me" is gone; the Writing introduction is "Essays, lessons from projects and personal stories.", and each essay keeps its own authorship label in the list and on its page. "Opinions I'm willing to defend" became "my honest take after trying things myself". Your Facebook Page is in the follow links. A stray pair of Markdown asterisks in one video description was found and is now cleaned automatically; a test scans every page for such marks.

How the homepage chooses

  • Featured piece: the item named in featured. If that is empty, mistyped or hidden: the newest full-length video.
  • Three projects: the ones named in homeProjects, in your order. Remaining places are filled with the newest projects that have a write-up, then projects without one. A picture is not required.
  • Never repeated: an item already shown, or the same subject in another form, is skipped. That is why the autonomous blog project is not among the three today: its video is the featured piece.
