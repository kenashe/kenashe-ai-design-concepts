Chapter IV

Behind the scenes

Four lessons from the making-of essay and its 4:49 walkthrough. Watch the film first if you’d rather see the story before the spoilers.

“I knew my actors needed character sheets. I didn’t expect my coffee mug to need one, too.”

From 4 Tips for Making AI Videos That Feel Like Films · Nov 20, 2026 · Human-directed, AI-assisted

Read the essay ↗YouTube ↗

1

Give recurring objects a reference sheet.

Watch from 0:12 ↗

The objects needed references for a different reason: they’re how you know whose memory you’re in. A man picks up a piece of pottery in the snow. Cut to the kitchen. You need to recognize that cup without anybody saying a word.

2

Tell the AI what to keep, not just what to change.

Watch from 1:36 ↗

The most useful part of my next note was “the original picture was fine.” Once I named what to preserve, I could ask for the small change I actually wanted.

In shot 15, the wind takes her hat. I liked the man’s reach, but he was looking to the side instead of at the brim.

3

Lock the pictures before you work on sound.

Watch from 2:32 ↗

I made a silent review copy with each shot number and its start time underneath the picture. I settled the pictures and their timing before trying the sound again. Getting a sound in sync and wanting to hear it are two different decisions.

4

Choose music for the story, not for every cut.

Watch from 3:44 ↗

My test was the hat. In summer, he puts it back on her head. In the next picture he’s walking alone, with the hat behind him in the snow. The pianist keeps playing. I didn’t need a new cue to tell me how to feel about that cut.

The composition and the performance have separate rights; Musopen released this particular recording into the public domain.
